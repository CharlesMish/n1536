What happens when three corners match?

Follow a warm connector from a target circle to the moving diamond with the same letter. Seat face ABC closes three connectors. The fourth now spans 4.067 units in space. Because RMS squares the four gaps, averages them and takes the square root, that lone gap gives an RMS of 4.067 ÷ 2 ≈ 2.033. The opening pose scored 1.894 with no seated face.

What stays the same

Four labels name the same corresponding vertices throughout. All six pairwise Euclidean distances are fixed. The target and its mirror are noncoplanar, and the six edge lengths are distinct. The moving body is never stretched or sheared.

Only this labeled pair is being compared. Permuting labels is a different matching problem. This study does not claim that every unlabeled shape, or every distance dataset, has two different hands.

The error is three-dimensional

RMS = √[Σᵢ ‖Rpᵢ − qᵢ‖² / 4]

Corresponding points are compared in all three coordinates, in fixed units. The orthographic projection is only a viewing device: screen overlap is not the test. In the centered views, zero translation minimizes the error for every rotation. Seating ABC instead translates the mirror until the three named vertices coincide.

The rotation and reflection cases share a fixed camera and scale. “Seat face ABC” uses a low view across that face at the same scale. Its fourth-point gap is twice the perpendicular height of D above ABC. The other three errors are zero, so the four-point RMS is half that gap.

Why the best rotation is certified

The centered target has covariance C = diag(4, 1, ¼). Its mirror reverses z. For any proper rotation R, put B = R diag(1, 1, −1). B is orthogonal with determinant −1, so tr B ≤ 1 and each diagonal entry is at most 1.

RMS² = 2 Σⱼ Cⱼⱼ(1 − Bⱼⱼ)
≥ ½(3 − tr B) ≥ 1

The identity rotation attains RMS = 1 exactly. “Best proper fit” uses that analytic solution; it does not stop an iterative search or infer impossibility from your attempt. The minimum includes every proper rotation and every translation.

The operation that changes the answer

“Allow reflection” reflects the original moving body through z = 0 and resets its rotation. This changes its orientation sign and aligns all four corresponding points exactly. The operation has determinant −1; it cannot be performed by a rigid turn in three dimensions.

With reflection allowed, the sliders still rotate that reflected body. “Best proper fit” returns to the original mirror pair and its rotation-only optimum. “Mirror” restores the opening attempt. There is no automatic motion.
